# Campaign research translated into v1

Primary-source review on 2026-08-31 focused on systems that improve a
Three-Kingdoms campaign without turning the sketch sim into a menu spreadsheet.

## What v1 adopted

1. **Terrain changes decisions.** Five battlefield biomes carry visible
   topography and unit-sensitive movement cost; rivers have controlled
   crossings rather than decorative water.
2. **Supply makes geography strategic.** Armies trace owned links to a capital
   or depot. Long routes add fatigue; severed routes lose men.
3. **Officers are the campaign's identity.** The 200-person almanac now has
   mutable levels, XP, skills, equipment, loyalty, injury, capture, defection,
   death and duels.
4. **Events are choices, not invisible modifiers.** Twelve deterministic Tales
   are queued into saves and require a visible affordable decision.
5. **Sieges have stages.** A fort assault deploys outside a sealed wall,
   focuses a real gate, then becomes courtyard/street combat after breach.
6. **Rivals need recognizable doctrine.** Seven data-defined strategic
   archetypes change reserve, massing, attack nerve, development and terrain
   preference without granting hidden rule-breaking bonuses.

## What v1 deliberately avoided

- repetitive officer-by-officer administration every season;
- a siege map that is only a statistical wall bonus;
- hidden random events that mutate the campaign without player agency;
- copying another game's UI, branding, data, text or art.

The result keeps this project's defining constraints: one click from
`file://`, classic scripts, deterministic simulation, Canvas 2D, paper palette
and boiling sketch line work.
